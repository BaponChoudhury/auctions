What happened to all 7,160 lots

Not every lot sells, and not every sale publishes a price. This is the part that shapes any analysis built on top.

Only 37% of lots publish a sale price, and the two houses differ by more than 2× on this (SDL 28%, Bond Wolfe 64%). Nearly a thousand more sold before the auction with no figure disclosed. Comparing sell-through between the two houses would mostly measure their disclosure policies, not their markets.

Bond Wolfe publishes no guide price on past results at all, so the "vs guide" column below exists only for SDL lots. Averaging that figure across both sources would produce a number that means nothing.

Where the lots are

Every lot is resolved from its postcode to a local authority and region — 99% of them. Both auction houses are Midlands-based, so the sample is heavily weighted there: that is a bias in this corpus, not a fact about the UK market.

Median sale runs from £45,000 in the North East to £293,000 in London — a 6.5× spread. That is why the pipeline now time-adjusts comps using each lot's own local-authority house-price index rather than one national figure.

Reading the columns

GuideThe published starting figure. It is a marketing number, not a valuation — lots routinely sell well above it.
Sold forThe hammer price. Excludes buyer's fees, which are charged on top.
vs guideHow far above the guide it went. The pipeline also computes a discount against Land Registry comparables — that needs the sales database loaded first.
ConditionClassified automatically from the listing text. Only filled in where the description was fetched; blank means not yet retrieved, not "no information".
TypeMapped onto Land Registry categories so lots can be compared like-for-like against real sold prices nearby.

Guides under £1,000 are excluded from the percentage figures: one parcel of land was guided at £1 and sold for £100 — real data, but a 9,900% uplift makes any average meaningless. The table shows the 150 largest sales, not all 2,679.

Bond Wolfe covers 10 of its 51 available past auctions here; SDL covers all 33. 16 properties were offered at both houses, and 271 postcode sectors are covered by both — which is what makes cross-source comparison possible.
